RAPTOR: adversarial patch transfer and operational risk on edge object detectors

A conference paper built on one part of my thesis. It asks how far an adversarial billboard patch transfers to other scenes and detectors, and whether the robot's hardware would notice the attack.

  • IEEE COINS 2026, Bologna
  • Lefkios Mavroudi, Antonis Savva, Christos Kyrkou, Theocharis Theocharides
  • KIOS Research and Innovation Centre of Excellence, GuardAI EU project

Problem

Robustness studies usually report one number: how much accuracy a patch removes from one detector. On a robot, what matters is also whether the patch works on detectors it was not optimised for, how much extra output it pushes into the rest of the stack, and whether anything on the device would flag it.

What I did

  • Optimised Expectation-over-Transformation (EOT) billboard patches against YOLOv8n in CARLA-GeAR, with a loss that pushes many raw predictions to high confidence.
  • Tested cross-scenario and black-box transfer to YOLOv8, YOLOv5, NanoDet+ and RT-DETR-L.
  • Profiled every model and scenario on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin: latency, FPS, power and energy per frame.
  • Compared preprocessing defences, including Local Gradient Smoothing (LGS).

Results

  • 80.6% → 49.2%YOLOv8n mAP@0.50, clean vs patched
  • ~40×more high-confidence false positives
  • 44 / 45model and scenario cases where latency and power stayed within normal variation
  • 63.6%less false-positive flooding with LGS

The main point of the paper: the perception system can be failing badly while the hardware looks normal, so monitoring latency or power would not catch this attack.
